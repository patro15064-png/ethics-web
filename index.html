01DEFINITION & REALITY

Cybercrime is crime committed with computers or the internet

A

Old crimes, new medium

Theft, fraud and threats use phones, email and browsers; online forms replace fake paperwork.

B

Targets ordinary people

Not just banks and companies, individuals too, often when their guard is down.

C

No break-in required

Criminals trick people into clicking a link or replying with a verification code.

02THE PATTERN LIBRARY

Three threats you are most likely to meet

Almost every consumer cyber incident starts with one of these three common tactics.

03 / 03
01↗

Phishing

GOAL

Steal passwords or banking credentials.

02↗

Identity theft

GOAL

Open loans, credit lines or drain assets.

03↗

Malware

GOAL

Hijack devices or extort ransom payments.

03THREAT 01 / PHISHING

Phishing is a fake message that looks real

It exploits trust and emotion, not device vulnerabilities.

01

Familiar sender

The name may look right while the address tells a different story.

02

Urgency

Pressure makes a quick click feel safer than a pause.

03

The trap

A link or attachment asks for the one thing it should never need.

“Legitimate organizations will not threaten account deletion over an unprompted SMS link.

04THREAT 02 / IDENTITY

Identity theft turns your details into someone else's tool

⌁

What thieves collect

Names, addresses, dates of birth, account numbers and the small details that make a fake story believable.

↗

How identity is used

Details can become a loan application, a new credit line, a fake support call or a way into an existing account.

◷

Delayed discovery

The first clue may arrive as a bill, a credit check or a notification you do not recognize.

The risk is not only what a criminal knows. It is what they can make someone else believe.

05THREAT 03 / MALWARE

Malware is software built to harm or spy

01

Viruses & Trojans

Malicious code can hide inside a file or pretend to be a useful app. Once opened, it may spread or invite more unwanted software.

02

Spyware & Keyloggers

Spyware watches activity. Keyloggers record what is typed, including passwords and private messages.

03

Ransomware

Ransomware locks files or systems and demands payment. Paying does not guarantee a safe return.

ROUTES IN Unexpected attachments · Fake updates · Untrusted downloads · Compromised accounts

06IMPACT

The cost is financial and personal

01

Financial damage

Stolen money, unauthorized purchases, recovery costs and time spent proving what happened.

02

Personal toll

Victims often find the loss of time, security and peace of mind more painful than the initial financial hit.

Anyone can be targeted, whatever their background, age or job.

07PREVENTION

Three habits block most attacks

Small pauses and routine updates remove the easiest openings.

01

Use unique, strong passwords

Use a password manager and 16+ character passphrases.

LOCK
02

Pause before you click

Check the sender, the request and the destination before you respond.

PAUSE
03

Apply system updates promptly

Updates close known gaps before they become someone else's shortcut.

PATCH
08INCIDENT RESPONSE

If something feels off, act fast

Speed matters: banks may be able to recall transfers within the first few hours.

1
STEP 1

Stop immediately

Do not click again, reply, or send more information. Screenshot the message if it helps you explain what happened.

2
STEP 2

Change credentials

Start with the affected account, then secure reused passwords and turn on multi-factor authentication.

3
STEP 3

Notify and report

Contact your bank or service, tell someone you trust, and report the incident through the relevant channel.
